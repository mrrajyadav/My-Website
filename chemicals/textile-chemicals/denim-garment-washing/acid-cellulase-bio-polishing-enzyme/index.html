1. Wet Processing Role & Technical Overview

Acid Cellulase Bio-Polishing Enzyme is a specialized chemical auxiliary belonging to the Bio-Polishing Enzymes classification within Garment Washing and Denim Chemicals. It is utilized primarily for bio-polishing applications across textile wet processing operations.

Functional Purpose: Engineered to deliver high-performance bio-polishing across 100% Cotton Knits, Viscose, Modal, Lyocell substrates in industrial textile manufacturing.

2. When Is It Used? (Processing Sequence)

Applied at pH 4.5–5.5 and 50°C–55°C in rotary washers or jet dyeing machines.

3. Why Is It Used? (Operational & Quality Challenges Solved)

Eliminates surface fuzziness and hairiness, imparting clean stitch clarity, smoother hand-feel, and permanent anti-pilling durability.

4. How Does It Work? (Chemical Mechanism & Mode of Action)

Hydrolyzes protruding weakened micro-fibrils from the main yarn body, which subsequently detach cleanly under mechanical tumbling shear.

5. Technical Selection Factors for Mill Buyers

Enzyme activity curve matching commercial washer temperature and pH operating range. Indigo contrast sharpness with effective anti-backstaining on white pocketing and weft. Controlled fabric weight loss and preservation of denim warp tensile strength. Compatibility with vintage wash aesthetics, laser engraving, and local fading techniques. Rapid wash-off and thermal/alkaline enzyme deactivation to halt fiber degradation

6. Chemical Compatibility & Co-Auxiliary Synergy

Acid cellulases require pH 4.5-5.5; neutral cellulases operate efficiently at pH 6.0-7.5. Avoid presence of heavy metals or residual chlorine bleaching agents which deactivate enzymes

7. Process Limitations & Operational Precautions

Over-dwell or failure to heat-kill cellulase enzymes triggers severe fabric strength loss. Precise liquor ratio and mechanical washer loading must be maintained per batch

8. Storage, Packaging & Handling Protocol

Store liquid and granular enzymes in cool conditions (5°C to 20°C). Avoid elevated storage temperatures that induce denaturation. Keep containers closed.
