1. Wet Processing Role & Technical Overview

Cotton Optical Brightener (Disulfonic Stilbene) is a specialized chemical auxiliary belonging to the Optical Brighteners classification within Optical Brighteners and Whitening Agents. It is utilized primarily for brightening applications across textile wet processing operations.

Functional Purpose: Engineered to deliver high-performance brightening across Cotton, Viscose, Linen substrates in industrial textile manufacturing.

2. When Is It Used? (Processing Sequence)

Added directly into hydrogen peroxide bleach baths or dedicated exhaust whitening cycles.

3. Why Is It Used? (Operational & Quality Challenges Solved)

Bleached cotton retains a faint yellowish cast; optical brighteners re-emit blue light to produce dazzling perceived whiteness.

4. How Does It Work? (Chemical Mechanism & Mode of Action)

Absorbs invisible ultraviolet radiation (340–380 nm) and fluoresces in the visible blue spectrum (420–460 nm), cancelling yellow undertones.

5. Technical Selection Factors for Mill Buyers

Fluorescent tint cast profile: brilliant neutral white, bluish-violet, or greenish hue. Substrate affinity matching target fibre (cellulosic, polyester, polyamide, acrylic). Chemical stability across alkaline hydrogen peroxide bleaching liquors and resin finishes. Light fastness and resistance to atmospheric phenolic gas yellowing during storage. Absence of greening effect threshold at recommended saturation dosage level

6. Chemical Compatibility & Co-Auxiliary Synergy

Cellulosic OBAs (stilbene derivatives) are anionic: incompatible with cationic softeners. Polyester OBAs (benzoxazole derivatives) require HT exhaust (130°C) or thermosol (180-210°C)

7. Process Limitations & Operational Precautions

Exceeding saturation concentration leads to self-quenching and dull greenish discoloration. Acid-sensitive stilbene types must not be applied below recommended bath pH limits

8. Storage, Packaging & Handling Protocol

Store in opaque, light-tight drums in a cool, dry area. Shield from direct sunlight and UV radiation to prevent photo-chemical degradation.
